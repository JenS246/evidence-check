Evidence Check

Evidence helps us understand what happened. Sometimes evidence tells us something directly. Other times, we have to draw a conclusion from what we know. And sometimes, we need to think about whether someone is repeating what another person said. Let's practice!

Round 1

Direct or Circumstantial?

Direct evidence establishes a fact without requiring an inference. Example: “I saw someone break the window.”

Circumstantial evidence requires an inference. Example: someone leaves a room with broken glass on their shoes just after a window breaks.

Round 1 complete

Next Up: Hearsay or Not?

Sometimes a witness tells us what someone else said. When a statement made outside of court is offered to prove that what the person said is true, it may be hearsay.

Not every statement made outside of court is hearsay. What matters is why the statement is being offered.

Activity complete

Nice Work!

You've practiced identifying direct evidence, circumstantial evidence, and potential hearsay. Remember, evidence doesn't always tell us everything directly. Sometimes we need to draw an inference, and sometimes we need to think about how someone's words are being used.

The three basics

Direct evidence
Establishes a fact without an inference. Example: a witness says, “I saw the car hit the fence.”
Circumstantial evidence
Requires an inference. Example: fresh tire tracks lead from the road to a damaged fence.
Potential hearsay
An out-of-court statement offered to prove what it says is true. Example: “My neighbor told me the store was closed,” offered to prove the store was closed.
